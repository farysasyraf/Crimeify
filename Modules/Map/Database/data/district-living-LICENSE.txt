district-living.csv and police-district-areas.csv: "Living here" on a police district's profile

district-living.csv: each administrative district's latest two years of household income and spending, poverty,
unemployment, schools and amenities, with its state's and Malaysia's for those years where they're published.
"php artisan map:update-living" makes it again from the sources below: run it when they're updated, then commit it.

Sources:   Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM), through OpenDOSM, https://open.dosm.gov.my/data-catalogue:
             hies_district, hies_state, hh_income, hh_poverty (household income, spending and poverty)
             lfs_district, lfs_state_sex, lfs_year (the labour force)
             hh_access_amenities (homes with piped water, sanitation and electricity)
           Ministry of Education, through data.gov.my: schools_district
Licence:   Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0), https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

As the sources have them:
  - Income is a household's gross monthly income, the median; spending is a household's monthly average. Malaysia's
    spending isn't in these files, so that comparison is left out.
  - Poverty is absolute poverty: the share of households under the poverty line.
  - Perlis, Kuala Lumpur, Labuan and Putrajaya are each one district. The jobs file gives only the state, which is
    theirs.
  - The files spell some districts differently, and differently from year to year, like Hulu Langat and Ulu Langat, or
    S.P.Utara and Seberang Perai Utara. The command joins them: see tidy() and config('map.living.aliases').
  - Perak's schools are for Larut, Matang and Selama together, under Larut dan Matang.

police-district-areas.csv: the administrative district each police district is matched to. It isn't the same thing:
police districts aren't published as areas, and they don't follow administrative districts, above all in cities.

Made once by finding which district shape each police district's pin (police-districts.csv) falls in, not from names:
all 155 pins fell inside a shape. Most police districts are in a district of their own name; many in cities share one,
like Petaling's five. A pin says where a police district's headquarters is, so a police district that crosses a
border gets the district its pin is in, like Ampang Jaya, in Hulu Langat.

Shapes:    geoBoundaries, Malaysia, ADM2 (2020), https://www.geoboundaries.org, Creative Commons Attribution 3.0. Only
           the result is kept here.

Changed by hand after that:
  - W.P. Putrajaya: the shapes have no Putrajaya, so its pin falls in Sepang. It's its own territory in the figures.
  - W.P. Labuan: the same.
  - Kulaijaya and Ledang: the shapes have the old names of Kulai and Tangkak.
  - Padawan is left out, so its profile has no "Living here". Its pin falls in Serian on these 2020 shapes, but Sarawak
    has made new districts since, like Siburan, so that can't be relied on.
  - Matu Daro's pin is in Daro, the district it's matched to. The police district takes in Matu too.
